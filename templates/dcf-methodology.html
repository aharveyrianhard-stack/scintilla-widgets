The model jumps straight from year-5 cash flow to a perpetual Gordon-growth terminal value — economically this assumes ROIC stays constant (or the market re-rates) the instant the explicit window ends.
Terminal / Stage IIIPerpetuity begins only once excess returns (ROIC − WACC) hit zero. Value = EBI/WACC — growth stops mattering because new investment earns exactly its cost of capital (NPV-neutral by construction).Classic Gordon growth: TV = UFCF₅×(1+g)/(WACC−g). Terminal growth g is a free slider (0-8%, default = risk-free 4.28%) — economically this assumes the firm keeps earning excess returns on ALL future growth, forever. Hard rule enforced: g < WACC.

Discount rateCAPM cost of equity (9.6%) blended with pre-tax cost of debt (5.5%) → WACC 9.5%. The structural gap that matters most: Morningstar's Stage II fade means a wide-moat company gets ~15-20 years of gradually-declining excess returns priced in before the model forces ROIC down to WACC. Scintilla's demo, with only a single 5-year stage, either (a) implicitly assumes those excess returns vanish immediately after year 5 (understating value for a true wide-moat compounder), or (b) bakes them into the terminal growth slider forever if g is set too high (overstating value, since Gordon growth then implies indefinite excess returns). Section 3 below builds the missing middle stage.

Reading the spread. A→B isolates the discount-rate effect: swapping the demo's raw 5-yr-regression beta (2.14, largely a reflection of NVDA's 2022-2025 volatility) for Morningstar's blended 9.5% WACC alone is worth about +$104/share — beta selection is the single biggest lever in this model, which is exactly what the demo's own tornado chart is built to show. B→C isolates the structural effect: holding the discount rate fixed and replacing the infinite-growth Gordon terminal value with a 15-year fade to zero-excess-return, then a no-growth-premium perpetuity, is worth about +$29/share here — Stage II/III together are 85.5% of total value (below), so how you model "after year 5" dominates the answer for a name still compounding revenue at 50%+. C→D is the remaining gap: Morningstar's own Stage I is more bullish in the back years than Street consensus (FY2031 revenue $856B modeled vs $1,005B consensus — Morningstar is actually more conservative on the top line, but earns it back through opex leverage and a full 10-year explicit build vs this demo's 5).

Formula

This is the piece a single-stage Gordon-growth model can't represent cleanly: value from year 21 onward is priced as if ROIC has fully converged to cost of capital — no excess-return premium — vs. the demo's Gordon terminal value, which (at any g > 0) still prices in excess returns forever.

4 · What "as best I can" means here — caveats

Stage I sourceUses the FMP demo's Street-consensus revenue path, not Morningstar's own (undisclosed, proprietary) year-by-year model — this is why Scenario C ($247.90) lands below Morningstar's filed $280.00 even with matching Stage II/III mechanics.
Stage II investment rateApplied Morningstar's disclosed 30.0% flat across all 15 fade years. Morningstar's real formula lets RONIC glide from NVDA's current ~90%+ ROIC down to 9.5% over the window, which would make the investment rate rise over time (more reinvestment needed as returns compress) rather than sit flat — 30% is presented as their own disclosed average, not re-derived here.
Perpetuity growthHeld EBI flat into Stage III (no terminal growth) since, mathematically, growth is value-neutral once ROIC=WACC — matches Morningstar's stated logic, sidesteps needing their undisclosed exact terminal EBI figure.
Not a recommendationBoth models are sensitive to beta/WACC choice (see A vs B, +$104/share) and to Stage I assumptions the analyst/tool sets by hand. Treat the $114-$280 range as a demonstration of methodology sensitivity, not a price target.
