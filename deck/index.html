What is in each slot, and the rules that protect it

Chart 1 — SPY. Chart 2 — SNDK. Not a guess: those are the two charts on the Apple TV wall you arranged by hand. Type over either field above, or use ?c2=auto to follow the loudest favourite.

The two charts are exactly half and half at every window size. The row is flex with two children at flex:1 1 0 — equal by construction, not by a breakpoint. v2 used a three-column grid with the areas "c1 c1 c2", which gave chart 1 two thirds. That is the split you saw on the Apple TV, and it is gone.

One timeframe bar, not two. It sits above and drives both charts. Inside the deck the charts run in bare mode, which also stops them writing the shared setting that used to make one chart change the other.

Scenes change charts, not the Station. LIVE remains the existing editable view. OVERNIGHT and INDEX LEADERSHIP are immutable starting points; editing one branches into remembered CUSTOM. COHORT FAVORITES reads the current Hub favorites and paginates every match without adding filler. Personal YouTube, SCINTILLA YouTube, and X stay mounted while chart scenes change.

Bottom row — personal YouTube, SCINTILLA YouTube, and X live. The two video panes are built for this box, not full pages squeezed into it: a thumbnail grid that counts its own columns from its own width, and a video that plays right there in the pane when you tap it. It still never picks one for you. Market-search video remains available as its own filter/surface; it is not a substitute for either YouTube identity.

X is a live capture, not a button. x.com refuses to be framed by anyone, permanently — so your own extension, X Feed Float 0.6.7, never framed it either. It captures the real logged-in tab and crops it. This pane does the same thing through the web platform's own door: pick your x.com tab once and its live pixels render here, scrolling as it scrolls, with zoom and pan to crop to the column. The one thing a web page cannot do is scroll that tab for you — only an extension can reach into another tab. For hands-off scrolling, X Feed Float is still the tool: Option+Shift+P.

Keep the scene direct. Each pane has one FULL/BACK path. Separate-window, generic PiP and standalone navigation were removed from this everyday scene after live use showed that they duplicated the fixed Station workflow.

Lose nothing. The PANES row lists every pane. On a phone the deck stacks and the live budget PAUSES panes behind a tap — it never removes one from the layout or the chip row.
