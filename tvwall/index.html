1 · The shape, in one paragraph

The Apple TV cannot run this by itself: tvOS ships no browser, and third-party TV apps are not allowed to render web pages — so "a wall app on the Apple TV" is a dead end, and anyone selling one is casting from another device anyway. The honest architecture: a Mac drives the wall, the Apple TV is just the screen. The Mac extends its desktop onto the TV over AirPlay (one Control Center click — Screen Mirroring → use as extended display; low-latency and plenty for dashboards) or, for the crispest permanent install, a plain HDMI cable. On that TV display, Chrome shows two kinds of panel: one hosted page from this surface carrying everything that may legally be embedded, and real Chrome windows — logged in as you — parked over reserved cutouts for the things that can't be embedded. That's the ChatGPT-PiP mental model done with window placement, which Hammerspoon already owns in our stack.

2 · The legal line

We never capture, proxy, or re-serve anyone's stream. Two clean lanes only. Lane A — sanctioned embeds: YouTube's IFrame player exists precisely so third-party pages can show YouTube inside YouTube's own player, ads and all — our /youtube widget already runs it. TradingView publishes embed widgets for the same reason. Our own widgets are ours. Lane B — browser excerpts: a real Chrome window showing x.com, signed in as you, displayed on a screen you own. That is not redistribution — it's a monitor. What we never do: screen-capture-and-restream, server-side scraping of feeds, or piping YouTube/X content through our own hosting. AirPlay itself stays clean because it's your Mac's local display pipe, not a re-push.

4 · First working version

One new hosted route, /tv — a scene page sized for the 1080p-class picture AirPlay negotiates: /pulse strip across the top, two YouTube IFrame panes stacked left, /heat or /chart as the center field, ticker tape on the floor, and a reserved cutout (right column, ~28%) drawn as a labeled dock: "X parks here." The page never pretends to show X — the real window covers that hole.

A fourth profile, tv, in scene_layouts — same percent-rect system the wall/laptop/narrow profiles already use. Adding or retuning the TV layout stays a DB row, not a deploy.

/handoff grows a TV section — connect the Apple TV from Control Center, then one Hammerspoon save does the rest: full-screen chromeless Chrome window of /tv on the TV display, plus the logged-in x.com/home (or X Pro) window placed exactly on the cutout geometry. Commands live in /handoff, per house style. Practical honesty: AirPlay wants one reconnect click per session, the Mac must stay awake, the TV's sleep timer goes off — and if the wall becomes furniture, the endgame is an HDMI run or a spare Mac mini behind the TV, which makes it pixel-perfect and always-on.

Proof: the route + scene row + handoff section land with receipts on the spine, as usual.
