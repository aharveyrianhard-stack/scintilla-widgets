2 · The context lens: the rule, written down

"You made some level of rule but a partial rule; it doesn't consider zooming… play around with the default zooms and rewrite the rules so it fits cleanly."
The rule, in order
  1. Only over the older four-fifths of the line on screen. Never over the newest fifth, and never right of it (the average names, the live price, the price scale). The fifth is now measured on the line that is actually drawn. Before, it was measured on the whole plot, which with the new right-hand margin was only about a tenth of the line.
  2. Never on the ticker badge, the Geiger chip, the deck's page arrows or its timeframe tag.
  3. The emptiest dark spot wins. It is judged from the chart's own pixels (price line, clouds, grid). A spot the price line runs through counts as a quarter of a box fuller, so an empty patch always beats a spot where the line runs through.
  4. It shrinks before it covers the price. If every spot at the usual size crosses the line, it tries one size smaller, then its smallest. Only when even that crosses does it take the spot the line crosses least.
  5. Bottom-left when it is about as empty as the best spot (within 5% of the box).
  6. It stays put through data refreshes unless somewhere is clearly (8%) emptier, or its spot now breaks rules 1 to 4.
  7. Zoom and pan: while the chart is moving, the lens holds still. 0.2 s after it stops, the lens is placed again by rules 1 to 6 from the new picture. This is cheap: during a 14-notch zoom on the two-chart page the chart's pixels were read 2 times instead of 28 (one read per chart after the zoom stopped, instead of one per notch per chart).
  8. No room at all: no lens, and the pane records why.

Code: _indicators/station-lens.mjs (placeLens, rules written at the top) and _indicators/lens-placement.mjs (lineTailBox, emptiestSpot). The same rule is checked by tests/station-zoom-fan-20260928.test.mjs.

3 · The Geiger chip takes the top right

"The system is having trouble with the locations of things, so as not to consume space… I feel like the Geiger should take the TOP RIGHT."

The chip now sits in the strip at the top of each chart, the same row as the ticker badge and above the plot. It lines up with the right edge of the plot, so it is left of the price scale and above the live price label. If the deck's timeframe tag is in the way it moves left, never onto the badge. If that row has no room (a very narrow chart), it drops just inside the plot's top-right corner. The chip therefore never covers the price, the clouds or the lens.

5 · The RSI fan: your full fan

"The oscillators look really weird to me. I don't think the oscillators include my RSI situation. Three hour, four hour, six hour, eight hour, 12 hour — that's not the full fan. And I only see one line in most of these."

What the full fan is (read from the Indicator Lab, nothing there changed)

The current Lab version is V4 (INDICATOR_LAB/sprints/2026-09-24-stack-alignment/rotation-output/RSI_MTF_Context_V4.pine, its review notes, and the "independent MTF" card on the Lab page):

  • six lines: RSI 14 on the 3H, 4H, 6H, 8H, 12H and daily bars, each computed on its own bars, with no smoothing (2H exists but is off by default);
  • the slow context cloud: at every bar, the band between the lowest and the highest of the 2-day, 3-day, weekly and 2-week RSI. These are four more RSIs, not moving averages. The Lab draws the band at 24% strength behind the lines. This cloud was the missing part. The Station drew the six lines only;
  • reference lines at 30, 50 and 70, and a name at the right end of each line.

Why most charts showed one line

  1. Missing data on futures. Measured today against the chart API: for ES and NQ it serves only about a month of intraday bars (1h: 485 bars from 30 Aug; 3h: 164; 4h: 254 from 30 Jul; 6h: 82; 12h: 45), and no 8h at all. The fan threw away the first 150 values of every line to let it settle, so 6H and 12H drew nothing ("—"), 8H said "n/a", and 3H/4H covered only the last few weeks. On a daily or 3-day chart that is a few pixels at the right edge. The only line across the chart was the daily one.
  2. Overlap and one colour. On stocks all six were there, but in one indigo shade and thin, and six RSIs between 40 and 60 drawn on a fixed 0–100 scale sat in a fifth of the panel. They looked like one blurred line.
  3. A stopped source. For stocks, the API's 8h bars stop on 18 Aug (and its 2D on 16 Aug), so 8H quietly ended six weeks ago.

What changed

  • The cloud is drawn: min–max of 2D, 3D, W and 2W RSI 14, in the Lab's own blue at 24%, behind the lines, and named in the legend ("2D–2W 55–69").
  • Missing widths are built from bars we already read, never invented: 8H = two 4H bars, 12H = three 4H bars, 6H = two 3H bars (joined on the Eastern clock the API itself uses), 2D = two sessions, 2W = two weeks. A built line is used only when the served one is missing, too short, or stops earlier. On ES that turns 8H from "n/a" into 51.9 and 12H from "—" into 53.7. On stocks 8H now reaches today.
  • Short histories still draw. With under 300 bars, a line settles over 42 to 84 bars instead of 150. Those values can sit a few tenths away from TradingView's, which starts at the listing, so the legend marks them "≈" (on ES today: 3H, 4H, 6H, 8H, 12H).
  • Colour follows the Station rule: each line is green when its RSI is at or above where it stood a day earlier, red when below. The five intraday RSIs usually move together, so they usually share a colour. The timeframe is therefore told by a strong ramp: 3H is a faint dotted line, 4H a light dashed one, then 6H, 8H and 12H are solid and each heavier and more solid than the last, and the daily line is the heaviest. Every line also has its name at its right end (3H, 4H, 6H, 8H, 12H, D). The first version of this branch used a weak ramp, and a reviewer found the five still read as one red bundle. The ramp was strengthened after that review.
  • Where the futures lines begin is marked. On ES/NQ the intraday lines start part-way across a daily or 3-day chart. A dotted rule and a small tag, e.g. "4H/8H/12H from 19 Aug" and "3H/6H from 8 Sep", show where each group starts. Before those points only the daily line and the cloud are drawn, so a lone daily line is no longer mistaken for the whole fan. Stocks have the full history and get no mark.
  • The panel's scale follows what is on screen (always showing 30 and 70, a little room either side), so the lines spread out instead of sitting in a thin strip.

7 · What could be wrong

  • The futures fan is only complete for about the last month: ES/NQ intraday lines only reach back that far (the API keeps a month of futures intraday bars), and they carry "≈". On a 6-month daily chart the daily line runs alone for roughly the first two-thirds; the dotted rules mark where the others begin (decision 3 would fix it).
  • Built lines vs TradingView: our 8H/12H/6H are joined on the Eastern clock from 00:00 (as the API cuts its own), and TradingView cuts intraday bars from the session open, so values can differ by a few points (this was already true of every intraday width; see the bar-rule note on each chart). The 2D pairs are counted from a fixed date without the holiday calendar, so a pair can differ from TradingView's by one day around a holiday.
  • Three lenses still cross the line: VST on TARGETS · DAY, and BE and VST on TARGETS · RSI. Those charts are filled from edge to edge at 6 months, and even the smallest lens has nowhere clear to go, so it takes the spot it crosses least.
  • Lens says STALE from 09:30 to about 10:05 every morning (seen in the in-session picture). This existed before today's change. The lens counts today's session as open at 09:30, but the first regular 30-minute bar only finishes at 10:00. Not changed here.
  • Intraday stepping: on 30m and 1h charts the slower lines are steps, because a line only moves when its own bar has finished. That is deliberate (no peeking ahead) and matches TradingView's history.
  • The 2-chart fan now asks the API for two more widths per chart (3D and weekly, for the cloud), and more 4H bars for building 8H/12H.
