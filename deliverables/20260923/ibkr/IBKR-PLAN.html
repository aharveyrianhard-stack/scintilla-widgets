3 · What to look at in your account (I never log in)

Client Portal → Settings → Account Settings → Market Data Subscriptions. Read only: do not press Subscribe, Configure or Continue while you are looking.

  1. Does it say Non-Professional? That is the difference between $2.00 and $19.50 a month for the index feed.
  2. In the subscription list, write down active or not, and the fee shown, for: NYSE Global Index Feed, US Securities Snapshot and Futures Value Bundle, and anything already listed for US stocks.
  3. Open any one of the three contracts in Trader Workstation — TICK-NYSE, TRIN-NYSE, AD-NYSE. If you are not entitled, IBKR's own Market Data Assistant pops up and names the subscription it wants. That single pop-up settles what we are guessing at above.
  4. Check that overnight trading permission is on, and whether market data for it costs anything on your account.
  5. Confirm the API acknowledgement is signed for the same username we will log the gateway in with. Entitlements follow the username, not the account.

5 · The weekly login, made impossible to miss

IBKR throws the login out roughly every seven days. If that happens quietly, the wall keeps drawing a line that stopped moving — the one outcome worth preventing. So the lane assumes it has failed unless it can prove otherwise.

What the lane saysWhenHow loudWhat you do
"IBKR login runs out soon"36 hours before it expires — Saturday morning, for a Sunday expiryphone push, and a strip across the top of the Station and the Hub, every hourSign in once whenever it suits you and nothing ever stops
"IBKR login has expired"the moment it lapsessame three places, every fifteen minutes, and the strip cannot be dismissedSign in now — the panes are frozen until you do
"IBKR is logged out"the gateway is not connected at allsameOpen the gateway, sign in, approve the push
"IBKR has gone quiet"nothing has arrived for six minutes while the market is opensameCheck the gateway is still up — this is the one that catches a silent death

Out of hours, quiet is not an alarm: the market being shut is not a fault. And the panes themselves say which one it is — a pane whose feed is logged out reads "ibkr gateway down" in words instead of sitting black.

Your steps, once a week, about a minute

  1. The push arrives on your phone, or the strip is across the top of the Station.
  2. Open IB Gateway (on the box: open the little web page it puts up, same screen).
  3. Type your username and password — or let it fill them in, which is what the login script in this branch is for.
  4. Approve the IBKR Mobile notification on your phone.
  5. The strip disappears by itself within a minute. Nothing else to do.

6 · How the numbers get to the wall

Exactly the way FMP's already do, so there is one path and not a second private one.

  1. IB Gateway holds the login. Nothing else ever sees your password.
  2. The reader opens a socket to it, asks for one-minute bars of the three contracts, and can place no order at all — the API is switched to read-only at the gateway, and the reader contains no order call of any kind.
  3. The minutes land in their own place: normalized/ibkr_internals_bars_v1. Breadth is not a share price, and the code refuses outright to write into a price store — that refusal is one of the tests.
  4. The chart API serves them on the same /candles route the Station already uses for everything else.
  5. The Station draws them as ordinary Scintilla charts: your greys, the cloud ribbon, hover in New York time, no caption.

One thing worth knowing about widths

We keep minutes, so any width can be built from them. I asked the chart API today for a seven-minute VIX and it refused — FMP publishes a fixed set of widths and that is not one of them. A seven-minute TICK will simply be served, because seven one-minute bars is all that takes. No timeframe gets refused.

8 · What I could not confirm, and will not pretend

  • Nothing has been proven against your actual account. Every contract above comes from IBKR's published documentation, not from a live answer. The first session with the gateway open settles all of it in about ten minutes.
  • Today's prices. IBKR's pricing page refuses to be read automatically, so the figures are from our own reading of it on 14 August 2026.
  • Which subscription actually unlocks the three contracts. The index feed is the strong candidate; IBKR's own pop-up will name it outright.
  • Which field on advance/decline carries what. Recorded for a full session before anything is published — not guessed.
  • The serving step. The chart API's source is not on this MacBook, so the reader here is a working, tested module plus an exact statement of where it plugs in, rather than a change I could make to that service.
  • IBKR's own position on running the gateway without a screen. They say a screenless gateway is unsupported. The container gives it an invisible screen, which is the usual approach and the one your earlier notes already chose, but it is their software and their rule.
