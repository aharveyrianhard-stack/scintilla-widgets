Charts only, and a lens that gets out of the way

Two things. One key gives the whole wall to the charts and takes the video and X panes off it without killing the X capture. And the Context Lens now moves to the side the price is not on, keeps off the newest prices, and holds still while you are reading it.

1 · Charts only

"The Station, like I said, I need a way to hide the YouTube and the Twitter to view the charts bigger."

Press W — for wall — or the charts only button in the dock. The video and X panes go, the charts take the whole wall, and the same key brings them back. The Station remembers which way you left it, in this browser: reload and it comes back charts-only.

Hidden, not unloaded — why the X capture survives

The obvious way to hide a pane is display:none. That would take the X pane's frame out of the page altogether, and the live capture with it — the thing the last night of work went into making reliable. So the media row is taken out of the layout (so the charts can have the space) and drawn at zero opacity behind them, at a real size. It is never unmounted.

Measured across a toggle at all three widths: the X pane is the same element with the same address, still in the page, display:flex, visibility:visible, opacity:0. Nothing reloaded.

What I could not prove: that a capture actually kept streaming, because no capture was running on this machine — the pane says "X SOURCE IS OFFLINE" in the screenshots. What is proven is that the pane is never destroyed or resized to nothing, which is what would break it. First time you use it on the real wall, glance at the X pane after bringing it back.

2 · The Context Lens moves out of the way

"Context lens workshop… if the chart is going up or down it needs to reposition itself to not block shit. I love it. I don't love the shape of it."

Three rules were added on top of "never cover the price line":

  • The side away from the action. The last third of the drawn line decides: rising puts the lens low, falling puts it high.
  • The newest prices are never covered. The last fifth of the chart is where you are looking, so nothing sits in it. If that leaves no room at all, the lens may enter that strip — but only while staying twice the usual margin clear of the prices in it, and it says so.
  • It does not jump while you are reading it. Your pointer on the lens pins it. Off it, it only moves when the new corner is meaningfully emptier, so an ordinary bar does not start it hopping.

4 · What could be wrong

  • The X capture claim is structural, not observed — see above.
  • Zero opacity is not free. The hidden panes are still being drawn, so a video left playing behind the charts still costs what it costs. That is the price of not tearing the X capture down; if you would rather the video stopped, that is a different switch and worth saying so.
  • W is a plain key. It does nothing while you are typing in a box, but if you use a keyboard shortcut elsewhere that ends in W, they will both fire.
  • The lens rule was checked on six saved windows, not on everything the Station shows. One of the six still parks on the shelf.
  • "Do not jump while being read" follows the pointer only. On a touch screen with no pointer resting anywhere, a new bar can still move it.
