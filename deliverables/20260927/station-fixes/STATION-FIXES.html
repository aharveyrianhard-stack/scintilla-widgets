3 · Speed, measured honestly

This was measured on a stand-in for the iMac: headless Chrome with the processor slowed 4×, empty browser storage, and the same recorded chart data for before and after, each request delayed 300 ms. There were 5 runs each, and the table shows the middle one. The iMac itself was not touched.

What this says, plainly.
  • On a cold start, nothing got faster. Prices come up at the same time. Clouds are the same or up to 0.25 s later, which is about the run-to-run spread. The lens lands by the time the last price draws.
  • The processor does 8% more work on AI 1 and 15% more on TARGETS · RSI, which now carries eight lenses it did not have. The cost is the search for the emptiest spot: it reads the chart's pixels about 2–3 times per chart per load. I cut that from 4 to 2.6 reads per chart and stopped it re-reading on every live price tick.
  • The clouds fix targets a wall that has been running, not a cold start. After one pass over 15 pages, the browser's store held 4.08 M characters before (about 80% of what fits) and 2.03 M after. The clouds' share went from 2.71 M to 0.66 M. On this short lap the old store had not overflowed yet, so I cannot show a timing gain. It removes the overflow that the full 75-name lap would hit.

Raw runs: cpu-runs.json. Scripts: harness/ (perf.mjs, lap.mjs, count.mjs, quota.mjs, evidence.mjs).

4 · Where each number comes from

  • Lens candles: the chart API's own 4-hour and 30-minute bars for that name (/candles?tf=240 and tf=30). One read per chart, repeated at most every 10 minutes. Nothing is computed from them except their position on screen.
  • The % on the crosshair: the cursor's price level against the same current price the badge and the line use: (level ÷ current − 1) × 100.
  • STALE: the same rules as before: a daily chart is stale from 09:45 New York if the previous session's bar is missing, a 3-day chart after 6 missing sessions, a weekly after 10, and intraday after two bars during 04:00–20:00. Nights, weekends and holidays are never judged.
  • The Geiger number: unchanged. The same reading from the same two owners as before; only where it sits changed.

5 · What could be wrong

  • "Emptiest" means least drawn on in pixels. A spot can be empty now and have the line grow into it later. The lens then moves, but only when another spot is clearly better.
  • On ES/NQ the RSI fan shows values for 3H, 4H and D only. 6H and 12H read "—" and 8H "n/a" (the chart API has no 8-hour futures series). The old SPY + QQQ · RSI page avoided this by always showing SPY/QQQ. This is existing fan behaviour on futures, not something new here.
  • The merged page's label still says "SPY + QQQ · DAY" when it shows ES/NQ. The badges name the real symbols.
  • The lens's timeframe tag stays because two lens timeframes now exist. You called it optional.
  • The 4× slowed Chrome is a stand-in. The real iMac may differ.

6 · What I did not do

  • No change to the Sunday/futures session mode, intraday for futures/commodities/Bitcoin, or macro intraday (your "do not change" line). The SPY/QQQ ↔ ES/NQ switch uses the existing clock, untouched.
  • No lens on MACRO · DAY (decision 2).
  • No push, no deploy, no database write, no paid service, and nothing run on the iMac.
  • The other two RSI twin pages (OTHER INDEXES, TARGETS) are still separate pages (decision 3).
  • Tests: 776 run, 758 pass. The 18 failures are exactly the 18 that already fail on the live Station code 1981096, by name. Nothing new fails.

7 · Decisions for you

  1. Keep the small 4H / 30M tag on the lens? Recommendation: keep it. It is the only sign of which candles you are looking at.
  2. 30-minute lens on MACRO · DAY? Recommendation: not yet. It would put futures and commodity intraday on screen, which you asked to plan first.
  3. Fold OTHER INDEXES · RSI and TARGETS · RSI into their day pages too (like SPY + QQQ)? Recommendation: yes. It saves two pages per lap and gives every daily page the same look.
